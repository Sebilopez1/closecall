Blind labeling: you only see the clip, never the AI's answer. Two people label the same clips so we can measure agreement.

CloseCall looks at places and patterns, never people: no face recognition, no license plates, no tracking anyone. Raw video stays in VAST; only events, decisions and labels are saved. Every action is approved by a person and logged.

How it decides. NVIDIA Cosmos describes each clip and YOLO11 marks every car, bus, truck, bike and person in each frame. CloseCall measures, from the point of view of the person on the bike or on foot, how close each vehicle came (in person-heights; about 2.5 is one car length), which side it came from, and whether Cosmos saw braking, swerving or a near miss.

Limits. Tested on a small hand-labeled set of street clips filmed from a car, standing in for a handlebar or helmet camera. Distances come from the camera image, so they're estimates. Cosmos and YOLO can miss fast, dark or hidden scenes, so unclear clips go to a person. Hazard tags (wires, holes, debris) are Cosmos's reading of the clip and aren't scored yet. Decisions are stored on the app's disk for the demo.
