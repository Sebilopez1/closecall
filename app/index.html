Blind labeling: you only see the clip, never the AI's answer. Two people label the same clips so we can measure agreement.

CloseCall looks at places and patterns, never people: no face recognition, no license plates, no tracking anyone. Raw video stays in VAST; only events, decisions and labels are saved. Every action is approved by a person and logged.

How it decides. NVIDIA Cosmos describes each clip and YOLO11 marks every car, bus, truck, bike and person in each frame. On the bike camera the rider is the camera: CloseCall follows each vehicle from frame to frame and uses its real size as a ruler (a car is about 1.5 m tall, a bus about 3 m) to estimate how far ahead it is and how much space it left beside the rider, in metres. A vehicle within about half a metre of the rider, less than 3 m ahead, is a close call; a big vehicle right alongside, too close to measure, goes to a person. On street and car cameras it checks how close vehicles come to anyone on a bike or on foot at the same depth, and whether Cosmos saw braking, swerving or a near miss.

Limits. Distances come from one camera, so they're estimates (roughly ±30–50%): they assume a forward-facing camera and typical vehicle sizes, and long vehicles seen from the side can't be measured. Tested on a small hand-labeled set of New York bike-camera clips plus some street and dashcam clips. Cosmos and YOLO can miss fast, dark or hidden scenes, so unclear clips go to a person. Hazard tags (wires, holes, debris) are Cosmos's reading of the clip and aren't scored yet. Decisions are stored on the app's disk for the demo.
